# NORTHSTAR — Company Context

## 1. Identity

NORTHSTAR (gonorthstar.com) is a Growth OS and Fractional CGO (Chief Growth Officer) for MiPymes and e-commerce businesses in Latin America. It is not positioned as a software tool, an agency, or a consultancy in the traditional sense — it is the growth operating system a company would build if it could afford to hire a top-tier commercial leadership team.

This identity — Growth OS, Fractional CGO, done-for-you execution today evolving toward AI agents over time — is NORTHSTAR's permanent frame. It does not shift as the product matures; only the mechanics underneath it do.

## 2. Mission

To give MiPymes a genuine "fighting chance" in digital markets. NORTHSTAR exists to democratize access to the strategies, frameworks, and technology that are normally reserved for companies large enough to afford C-level growth talent. The people who build great products deserve a system that pushes them to win — not one that quietly lets them lose to better-resourced competitors.

The underlying belief: founders should spend their energy on the product they love. NORTHSTAR exists to take on the commercial, analytical, and operational weight around that product.

## 3. The Problem NORTHSTAR Solves

Across Latin America, millions of MiPymes sell through digital channels (Shopify, Amazon, Mercado Libre, Tiendanube) but operate with almost no visibility into their own numbers. This shows up as three compounding pains:

- **Operational pain**: Founders sink enormous time into platforms they don't fully understand, leaving configurations at a basic, underoptimized level. Poor response systems mean they lose customers to silence and slow service.
- **Strategic pain**: Pricing is copied from competitors rather than derived from the business's own margins and economics, which risks price wars the business can't win. This blindness pushes them toward the bottom of marketplace search results.
- **Financial pain**: These businesses cannot afford to hire a CGO or a premium consultancy, so they never get access to the frameworks, metrics, and methodologies that would otherwise professionalize their growth.

The founder ends up simultaneously acting as CEO, warehouse worker, and customer service rep — working *in* the business, never *on* it.

## 4. Who NORTHSTAR Serves

NORTHSTAR's current (non-future) ICP has two archetypes. Both are e-commerce businesses; a third archetype (short/mid-term rental properties) exists in the broader company vision but is explicitly out of scope for the current phase and should not shape product or report design right now.

### Archetype 1 — El Operador Estancado (The Stalled Operator)
An e-commerce business with real product-market fit and real sales, but stuck below its potential. Typically over-dependent on a single channel (Amazon or Mercado Libre), or has an owned store (Shopify/Tiendanube) but struggles to drive traffic to it. Prices by copying competitors rather than understanding their own unit economics. The founder is doing everything themselves.

- What moves them: platform fee increases eating their margin, watching competitors expand into new channels, burnout from manual operations.
- What they need to hear: "We see your potential, and here's the operator who will get you there."
- Where NORTHSTAR's help lands hardest: channel diversification, pricing intelligence, margin recovery, operational leverage.

### Archetype 2 — El Catalizador Digital (The Digital Catalyst)
An offline business — often generating serious revenue already — that is nearly invisible online. The person driving this initiative is usually the founder, or an internal champion (commercial director, marketing lead, next-generation family member) tasked with "digitizing" the company. They've often already paid an agency for branding and social presence that produced no measurable revenue, and they're paralyzed by the sheer number of platform, ERP, and logistics decisions in front of them.

- What moves them: pressure from ownership/the board to show online revenue, competitors with inferior products winning through better digital execution, a bad prior agency experience.
- What they need to hear: "Your offline success proves the model works. We'll build and run the digital arm at a corporate level, without pulling your team's focus off the core business."
- Where NORTHSTAR's help lands hardest: digital architecture built from zero, realistic revenue projections for new channels, clear lines around what NORTHSTAR operates versus what the client owns.

### (Future, not current) Archetype 3 — El Maximizador de Rendimiento
Owners of short/mid-term rental properties who set static prices without market data and are underserved by property managers who take 20–25% and deliver no real revenue strategy. This archetype is part of NORTHSTAR's long-term vision but is not part of the current product scope, ICP targeting, or report design.

## 5. What NORTHSTAR Delivers

The tangible value NORTHSTAR provides today runs on a recurring diagnostic cycle:

- **Periodic Diagnosis**: A business-health report built from the client's own data, showing period-over-period movement.
- **Prioritization**: Rather than drowning the client in every metric, NORTHSTAR identifies the handful of highest-impact opportunities to act on next.
- **Divided Execution**: NORTHSTAR and the client split the work. NORTHSTAR handles the digital/commercial side it can execute directly — pricing adjustments, marketplace advertising, listing updates, catalog work. The client handles what only they can do — new products, packaging, manufacturing, physical quality. The shorthand: *"You make the product, we sell it."*
- **Results Review**: A look back at what changed and what it produced, closing the loop before the next cycle begins.

NORTHSTAR today operates human-in-the-loop by design: the NORTHSTAR team reviews data-driven suggestions and executes the changes it's responsible for manually. This is the current operating model, full stop — it is simply how NORTHSTAR works right now.

## 6. Business Model (Conceptual)

NORTHSTAR is built on a "skin in the game" philosophy: if the client grows, NORTHSTAR grows. Three components make up the commercial relationship:

- **Platform Fee**: A fixed monthly fee for access to and maintenance of the system.
- **Revenue Share**: A flat percentage of net sales on the channels NORTHSTAR operates, billed monthly. The percentage is negotiated per client by the CEO and agreed before any work begins, around 7.5% being typical, so both sides know the number from the outset.
- **Setup Fee**: A one-time charge for clients who need their digital channels built from scratch. This is a commercial arrangement handled outside of any software system.

NORTHSTAR does not sell "technology integrations" or "consulting" — messaging always targets the specific asymmetry or friction each archetype feels (the gap between product quality and commercial performance; the risk of continued inaction and wasted agency spend), not the tools used to fix it.

## 7. Influences and Operating Frameworks

NORTHSTAR's strategy and communication style draw on a specific set of playbooks:

- **Copywriting & sales** (Isra Bravo): "Pretty marketing = poor business." Sell outcomes, not features. Pain before pleasure; project zero neediness.
- **Attention & distribution** (Gary Vaynerchuk, Seth Godin): Find the exact tribe with the specific problem (Minimum Viable Audience) and meet them where they already consume content.
- **Growth & scaling** (Brian Balfour / Reforge, HubSpot): Build growth loops where retention funds acquisition.
- **Conversion thinking** (Dave McClure — AARRR): Acquisition, Activation, Retention, Revenue, Referral — optimized around revenue, not vanity metrics like visits.
- **Execution philosophy** (Paul Graham / Y Combinator): "Do things that don't scale." NORTHSTAR executes manually today to deliver immediate value, while that manual work trains the automation of tomorrow.

## 8. Ecosystem

**E-Commerce Master by NORTHSTAR Academy** is an 8-week educational program for founders who aren't yet ready for the full Growth OS, or who need to understand their own business fundamentals (vision, purpose, competitive advantage, go-to-market, value-based pricing, omnichannel, and data) before connecting to NORTHSTAR's system. It functions both as an entry product and as a filter that pre-educates clients before they onboard onto the platform.

## 9. Brand and Design Sensibility

NORTHSTAR's product should feel professional and calm enough that a marketing manager or a business owner — not just a technical operator — is comfortable living in it daily. The internal design reference point is Palantir: a mix of dark and light surfaces that reads as technically credible without tipping into cold or intimidating, with restrained neon accents used deliberately rather than decoratively. The product should never look "vibe-coded" — no gratuitous gradients, no harsh pure-black surfaces, no generic developer-tool aesthetic.

## 10. Geography

NORTHSTAR is launching in Mexico first. The expansion sequence after that prioritizes other LatAm countries with the largest e-commerce markets, since NORTHSTAR intends to become the biggest player in the region.

## 11. Stage and Trajectory

NORTHSTAR is very early — a small handful of real paying clients today, with a near-term goal of reaching dozens of clients, not hundreds. Every part of the product should be built to make onboarding, running, and reporting on those dozens of clients excellent, rather than prematurely building for scale the company doesn't yet have.
